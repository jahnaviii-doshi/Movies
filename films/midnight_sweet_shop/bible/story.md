# PART A — CREATIVE BRIEF

## A1. Logline
On sweltering summer nights in an old Indian town, a shy girl discovers a sweet shop that appears only at midnight — where sweets work magic and the only currency is a kindness done that day. But the shop's ancient keeper is fading, because the world has forgotten her, and the girl's greedy cousin is about to make it worse.

## A2. The two layers
- **Kid layer:** magic sweets (float, be brave, talk to animals), a funny dog, a show-off cousin who gets his sticky comeuppance, a big rescue at midnight.
- **Adult layer:** memory and disappearance. Old crafts, old shops and old people vanish when nobody remembers them. Grandmother half-remembers a story she lived as a child; the film is about getting the ending back. Never state this outright — let the fading shopkeeper and Nani's lost memory carry it.

## A3. Influences (techniques only — the story, characters and world are entirely original)
Classic British children's-fantasy techniques: children with real agency while adults are asleep or busy; magic sitting right next to the ordinary world; a world with clear rules and a ticking clock; food as joy and comfort; clear moral payback for kindness and greed; eccentric magical adults.

## A4. Setting
**Chandpur** (fictional), a small old town in North India. A narrow *gali* of old havelis with carved wooden balconies, tangled electric wires, potted tulsi, sleeping cycle-rickshaws. Summer holidays, power cuts, families sleeping on charpais on the rooftop under the stars. At the dead end of the gali: a blank, peeling blue wall that nobody looks at.

## A5. Characters (keep cast small — this matters for consistency)
| Name | Who | Arc / function |
|---|---|---|
| **Meher** (9) | Quiet, observant, kind; braids, oversized kurta, one missing front tooth | From timid to the one who holds the memory |
| **Kabir** (11) | Her cousin visiting from the city; cap backwards, show-off, wants to impress | From faking goodness to a real one — confession is his kind deed |
| **Chutney** | A one-eared street dog, scruffy, ochre and white, huge loyalty | Leads Meher to the shop; comic relief; one spoken line under the animal-talk sweet |
| **Mishri Amma** | The shopkeeper — tiny, ancient, round spectacles, a shawl that seems spun from sugar-thread; slightly translucent and fading more each night | The disappearing past; warm, funny, firm about rules |
| **Tola** | The shop's brass weighing scale — has a face in its engraving, expressive pans, communicates by clanks and tilts (no words) | Weighs kind deeds; comic and moral judge |
| **Nani** (70s) | Meher's grandmother; tells half a story every summer night but can't remember the ending | The adult anchor; her remembering is the climax |

## A6. World rules (show, never lecture — each rule is introduced through action)
1. The shop appears only at the stroke of midnight on summer nights, unfolding out of the blank blue wall.
2. You pay with **one true kindness done that day**. You put your hand on Tola's pan and the kindness appears as a glowing memory-bubble. Fake or boastful deeds weigh nothing.
3. A sweet's magic lasts until first light.
4. Take without paying and the sweet's sugar takes *you* — you slowly start turning to crystal sugar.
5. The shop — and Amma — exist only as long as someone remembers them.

## A7. The magic sweets (visual set-pieces)
- **Floating laddoo** — you drift up like a lantern (rooftop flying sequence).
- **Brave toffee** — your shadow grows tall and heroic before you do.
- **Kulfi of tongues** — you understand animals for one hour (Chutney gets one line — make it funny and heartfelt).
- **Wish jalebis** — kept in a locked jar, forbidden; each coil grants a small wish that goes sideways.

## A8. Beat sheet (target timings — runtime 20:00)
1. **00:00–01:30 — Summer night.** Power cut; family on the rooftop; Nani tells "the story of the midnight sweet shop, where you pay with goodness…" and loses the ending again. Kabir mocks it. Meher believes.
2. **01:30–03:30 — Midnight.** Chutney whines at the gali; Meher follows. The blue wall glows; the shop unfolds — shelves, lanterns, sweets that glow. Mishri Amma, faintly see-through: "You're late. Eighty years late."
3. **03:30–06:00 — The rules.** Meher has no money. Amma points at Tola. Meher's kindness (she shared her water with Chutney in the heat) floats up as a bubble; Tola tips; the shop chimes. Floating laddoo → joyous flight over the moonlit rooftops of Chandpur.
4. **06:00–08:00 — Kabir comes along.** The next night he invents a grand kindness. Tola doesn't move. Amma is kind but firm. Kabir is humiliated and angry.
5. **08:00–10:30 — Montage of nights.** By day Meher does small real kindnesses (helps the ironing-wala, waters the neighbour's plants, reads to a sleepy toddler). By night: brave toffee, kulfi of tongues (Chutney's line). Each night Amma is fainter and the shelves emptier. Meher notices; Amma brushes it off.
6. **10:30–12:30 — The theft.** Kabir sneaks in and steals the jar of wish jalebis to show off to the gali kids. Wishes go comically wrong — sugar-syrup floods the gali, a rickshaw flies, a buffalo turns pink. Then horror: Kabir's fingertips start turning into crystal sugar.
7. **12:30–15:00 — Low point.** That night the shop barely appears — a flicker in the wall. Amma is almost gone. Kabir confesses, crying, but has no kindness to pay with. Meher realises rule 5: Amma is fading because *no one remembers her*.
8. **15:00–17:30 — Climax.** Meher and Kabir wake Nani and bring her to the wall at midnight. Kabir carries her down the stairs — his first real kindness (Tola later weighs it heavy). Nani sees the shop… and remembers: she was the girl in the story; she once saved Amma too. Her memory floats onto Tola's pan, and the shop blazes back to full colour. Kabir's sugar-crystal fingers turn back to skin.
9. **17:30–19:00 — Dawn.** The shop folds back into the wall. Amma, solid again, gives Meher something small (a single sugar-thread from her shawl) — the job of remembering passes on. Amma: "Don't let the story lose its ending."
10. **19:00–20:00 — Coda.** Next summer, rooftop, power cut: Meher tells the story to the younger kids — and this time she knows the ending. Final shot: down the gali, the blue wall shimmers at midnight. Credits. Post-credits gag: Chutney sneaks a laddoo and floats away, legs paddling.

## A9. Visual style
Warm, hand-painted 2.5D look: soft painterly textures, rounded, simple character shapes with big expressive eyes (simple shapes = better consistency). Night palette of deep indigo and blue-black, lit by the **glow of the sweets** — saffron, rose pink, pistachio green, silver-leaf white. Daytime: dusty ochre and whitewash under harsh summer light. The shop is the warmest, most saturated place in the film. Amma's fading shown by translucency and desaturation.
Camera: gentle and storybook-like — slow push-ins, wide rooftop vistas, low child-height angles in the gali.

## A10. Sound & music
Instruments: santoor, bansuri, sarangi, harmonium, light tabla, soft strings.
Leitmotifs: **(1) Amma's lullaby** — a simple 6-note tune Nani hums in scene 1 without knowing why; it becomes the climax theme. **(2) Wonder theme** — the shop unfolding / flying. **(3) Kabir's swagger** — playful, cocky; turns tender in the climax.
Soundscape: night crickets, distant train horn, ceiling fans stopping in the power cut, the shop's wind-chime "hello", Tola's clanks.

## A11. Tone guardrails
Funny first, then tender. Peril is mild and short. Kabir is never a villain — just a kid who wants to be liked. Nani's memory loss is gentle and dreamy, not clinical.
