# THE MIDNIGHT SWEET SHOP

*An original animated short film. Runtime 20:00.*

**CAST**
MEHER (9). Quiet, watchful, kind. Two braids with red ribbons, a mustard kurta three sizes too big, a gap where a front tooth used to be.
KABIR (11). Her cousin from the city. Red cap worn backwards, lightning-bolt T-shirt, a grin that is always auditioning.
CHUTNEY. A one-eared street dog, ochre and white. Mostly tongue and loyalty.
MISHRI AMMA. Tiny, ancient keeper of the sweet shop. Round spectacles, a bun like a cloud, a shawl spun from pink sugar-thread. She is faintly see-through, and a little more so each night.
TOLA. The shop's brass weighing scale. A face is engraved on its centre plate. It talks in clanks and tilts.
NANI (SHARDA) (70s). Meher's grandmother. Silver bangles, a lavender-bordered sari, and every summer half a story.

---

## 1. SUMMER NIGHT — 00:00–01:30

**EXT. CHANDPUR — DUSK**

A small old town, baked all day, still exhaling heat. Domes, water tanks, laundry lines. Heat shimmers above the rooftops. In a hundred windows, ceiling fans turn.

TITLE CARD: *The Midnight Sweet Shop*

The town glows orange as the sun sinks into dust. Then, like candles pinched out one by one, every window goes dark.

The fans sigh to a stop. *Wuuuh...*

A chorus of groans rises from the lanes below. A distant train horn answers.

**EXT. NANI'S ROOFTOP — NIGHT**

Stars, more of them than a city child would believe. Three charpais, a water tank, a pot of tulsi, a hurricane lantern.

NANI sits propped against pillows and fans herself slowly with a woven fan. MEHER lies on her stomach with her chin on her fists, all ears. KABIR sprawls and fans himself with his cap, as bored as he knows how to be.

> **NANI**
> Once, in this very town, there was a sweet shop that opened only at midnight.

Meher's eyes widen.

> **NANI**
> And you could not pay with money there. Only with goodness.

> **MEHER**
> Then what happened?

Nani opens her mouth to answer. Nothing comes. Her fan slows.

> **NANI**
> Then... hm. The ending's gone again. Like a sweet you left in your pocket.

> **KABIR**
> Because there *is* no ending. It's made up.

Nani doesn't argue. She lies back and hums six notes, a lullaby she couldn't tell you the name of. (**AMMA'S LULLABY**)

Meher rolls to the parapet. Below, the gali is a dark crack between old houses, all carved balconies and tangled wires. At its dead end, a blank, peeling **blue wall** catches the moonlight.

Meher looks at it longer than anyone ever has.

---

## 2. MIDNIGHT — 01:30–03:30

**EXT. ROOFTOP — LATER**

Everyone is asleep. Nani snores softly, and Kabir's cap is over his face.

Far away, a clock tower strikes. *Dong... dong...*

Meher's eyes open.

From the gali below, a thin, hopeful **whine**.

**EXT. GALI — CONTINUOUS (MEHER'S POV, LOOKING DOWN)**

CHUTNEY, a scruffy one-eared dog, stands in the lane looking straight up at her. His single ear is up. His tail goes like a metronome.

He trots three steps toward the dead end, looks back, and trots three more. *Come on.*

**INT. STAIRWELL — NIGHT**

A narrow staircase lit only by moonlight through a jali screen. Meher tiptoes down, barefoot, holding her breath on the step that creaks.

**EXT. GALI — NIGHT**

Low and child-high, we follow Meher past a sleeping cycle-rickshaw, under a canopy of black wires, past doors with brass knockers shaped like lions.

Chutney sits facing the blue wall like it's a television.

> **MEHER** *(whispering)*
> It's just a wall, Chutney.

The last chime of midnight fades.

A crack in the peeling paint begins to **glow gold**. Then another, and another, until the cracks join into a shape: a doorway.

A wind chime rings, *ting-a-ling*, with no wind. (**SHOP HELLO**)

**THE SHOP UNFOLDS.** (**WONDER THEME**)

Like a pop-up book opening, the wall folds out in panels. A striped awning drops and snaps taut. Shutters swing open. Lanterns blink alight one by one. Shelves slide out of nowhere, crowded with jars, and every jar **glows**: saffron, rose pink, pistachio green, and the white of silver leaf.

Chutney sneezes from sheer joy.

**INT. THE MIDNIGHT SWEET SHOP — CONTINUOUS**

Warm and gold, smelling (we can tell) of cardamom and ghee. Laddoos stacked in pyramids, barfi glinting under silver leaf, jalebis coiled like sleeping snakes. On the counter stands TOLA, a polished brass scale with a face engraved in its centre plate. Its eyes are closed. It is asleep.

Meher steps inside. The floorboards sigh.

> **VOICE (O.S.)**
> You're late.

Meher jumps. Over the counter rise a pair of round spectacles, then a cloud of white hair, then MISHRI AMMA, so tiny she must be standing on a stool. The lantern-light shows faintly *through* her.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> Eighty years late.

> **MEHER**
> I'm only nine.

Amma peers at her over the spectacles.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> Then you're very early for someone so late.

---

## 3. THE RULES — 03:30–06:00

**INT. SHOP — CONTINUOUS**

Meher drifts along the shelves like a moth. A jar of golden laddoos pulses softly, and the laddoos inside bob up against the lid.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> Floating laddoo. Very popular.

Meher checks her pockets: one button and a rubber band. Her face falls.

> **MEHER**
> I don't have any money.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> Money?

Amma laughs, a wheezy, delighted laugh. Tola wakes and rattles its pans like it's laughing too. *Clinkle-clank.*

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> We don't take money here. Tola weighs something heavier.

Tola tilts one empty pan toward Meher, polite as a waiter.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> Put your hand on the pan. Think of one kind thing you did today. A true one.

Meher hesitates, then lays her palm on the cold brass.

From under her hand, a **glowing bubble** rises, a memory floating in light. Inside it:

**MEMORY (INSIDE THE BUBBLE) — EXT. GALI — DAY**

Harsh noon. Heat haze. Chutney pants in a thin strip of shade. Meher, sweating herself, looks at her water bottle, then pours it into her cupped hands, and the dog drinks.

**BACK TO SCENE**

The bubble settles gently onto the pan.

Tola's eyes open. The pan sinks. **CLONK.**

Every jar in the shop rings at once, a bright chord. Chutney barks twice.

Amma lifts a single laddoo with silver tongs and sets it on a leaf.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> One floating laddoo. It lasts until first light.

She points one bony finger at the sky.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> Not one minute more.

Meher takes a bite. Her eyes go round. Her heels lift off the floor, then her toes. She grabs a shelf, giggles, lets go...

**EXT. GALI — CONTINUOUS**

...and drifts out of the door and **up**, rising like a sky lantern. Chutney bounds below her, barking. Amma watches from the doorway, smiling.

**EXT. SKY OVER CHANDPUR — NIGHT** (**WONDER THEME**, full)

Meher flies. She tips forward and glides over moonlit rooftops: water tanks, pigeon coops, a kite tangled in wires, families asleep on their roofs like cargo under the stars.

She swoops over her own roof. Nani and Kabir are asleep below her, and she waves at them though nobody sees.

She turns a slow somersault in front of the moon.

**EXT. NANI'S ROOFTOP — FIRST LIGHT**

The sky blushes pink at its edge. Meher sinks, light as a dropped feather, onto her charpai. Her feet touch. Her weight comes back. She lies there grinning at nothing.

---

## 4. KABIR COMES ALONG — 06:00–08:00

**EXT. ROOFTOP — MORNING**

Kabir sits up and squints at Meher. A few golden crumbs sparkle on her kurta.

> **KABIR**
> You were floating. I saw you.

Meher says nothing. Then she smiles her gap-toothed smile, which is worse.

**EXT. BLUE WALL — THE NEXT MIDNIGHT**

Kabir, Meher and Chutney stand before the wall. Kabir's arms are folded.

> **KABIR**
> If this is a joke—

The cracks glow. The shop unfolds around them. Kabir's arms un-fold.

**INT. SHOP — CONTINUOUS** (**KABIR'S SWAGGER** theme)

Kabir strolls in as if he owns the place, cap backwards, thumbs in his pockets.

> **KABIR**
> I'll have the best thing you've got.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> Pay first.

He slaps his hand onto Tola's pan.

> **KABIR**
> Today I saved a whole busload of children from a flood. With a rope. And a helicopter.

A bubble wobbles up from his hand. Inside: a crayon scribble of a bus, a helicopter drawn by a five-year-old. It drifts down onto the pan...

...and weighs nothing. The pan doesn't move. Tola gives one small, unimpressed *tink*.

> **KABIR**
> It's broken.

He pushes the pan down with one finger. It springs back up and boinks him on the nose. *BOING.*

Meher snorts, then covers her mouth.

> **MISHRI AMMA** *(gently)*
> Tola only weighs what's true, little man. Come back when you've done something real.

Kabir's ears go red.

> **KABIR**
> It's a stupid shop anyway. A stupid sugar museum.

He storms out into the dark. Amma watches him go. She isn't angry.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> That one's hungry for something. And it isn't sweets.

Meher notices a bare patch on a shelf: a ring in the dust where a jar used to stand.

---

## 5. A MONTAGE OF NIGHTS — 08:00–10:30

(**WONDER THEME** reprised as a light, bouncing tune)

**EXT. GALI — DAY**
Meher staggers under a tower of folded laundry and delivers it to the ironing cart. A wrinkled hand reaches out and gives her a thumbs-up through a puff of steam.

**EXT. NEIGHBOUR'S BALCONY — DAY**
Meher waters a row of wilted marigolds with a dented brass lota. As the water reaches them, they lift their heads.

**INT. NANI'S HOUSE — AFTERNOON**
A toddler's head droops onto Meher's lap while she reads aloud from a picture book. She keeps reading, softer and softer.

**INT. SHOP — NIGHT**
Bubble. Pan. *CLONK.* The jars chime. Amma hands over a twist of shiny paper: a **brave toffee**.

**EXT. NANI'S ROOFTOP — NIGHT**
A monkey on the water tank holds Nani's spectacles to its own face. It is very pleased with itself.

Meher eats the toffee. Her **shadow** on the wall behind her grows first: taller, broader, its braids flaring like a hero's cape. Meher herself is a beat behind, still nine and small. She plants her feet.

The monkey sees the giant shadow, drops the spectacles, and flees in a scramble of tails.

Meher catches the spectacles. Her shadow gives her a thumbs-up.

**INT. SHOP — NIGHT**
Bubble. Pan. *CLONK*, but the chime is thinner this time. Amma is fainter. The shelves behind her are emptier. She hands down a small clay cup: **kulfi of tongues**.

**EXT. GALI — NIGHT**
Meher and Chutney sit on the step of the sleeping rickshaw, sharing the kulfi. Chutney licks his nose clean, looks up at her, and finally speaks: earnest, gravelly, honest.

> **CHUTNEY**
> You gave me your water when you were thirsty too, so I'll guard you forever... also I ate your slipper last week. Sorry.

Meher laughs until she has to hold her stomach. Chutney wags so hard his whole back half wags.

**INT. SHOP — NIGHT**
Meher counts the jars: there are fewer. Amma's hand passes over a lantern, and the lantern shows straight through it.

> **MEHER**
> Amma, where are all the sweets going?

> **MISHRI AMMA** *(airy)*
> Oh, sweets come, sweets go.

Amma waves it away. Her hand flickers like a candle in a draught.

---

## 6. THE THEFT — 10:30–12:30

**INT. SHOP — NIGHT**

Amma dozes on her stool. Meher kneels to scratch Chutney's ear.

In the doorway, a backwards red cap.

Kabir slips in. He looks up. On the highest shelf sits a tall jar of **wish jalebis**, coiled, glowing violet, held shut by a tiny brass padlock. He climbs the shelf, one silent hand over another.

Tola's eyes snap open. It clanks: *CLANK-CLANK-CLANK!*

Amma snores. Meher looks up just in time to see the doorway empty, and the high shelf empty too.

**EXT. GALI — NEXT DAY**

Bright, hot and dusty. Kabir stands on an upturned crate, the jar under his arm, the padlock broken. He addresses an audience we hear but never see: giggles, gasps, "Show us!"

> **KABIR**
> Wish jalebis. One coil, one wish. Watch this.

He bites a coil.

> **KABIR**
> I wish this gali was the sweetest place in the world!

A pause. Then **golden syrup** gushes out of every drainpipe and window box. In seconds the lane is ankle-deep in it, glossy and amber. A slipper floats by. Kabir wades in it, delighted.

> **KABIR**
> I wish I had the fastest ride in town!

The sleeping cycle-rickshaw **rises off the ground**, spins once, and rockets down the gali with Kabir clinging to the seat and yelling. It loops the electricity pole and plops back down in the syrup. *SPLOOSH.*

> **VOICE (O.S.)** *(a little kid)*
> Make the buffalo pretty!

Kabir, dizzy and grinning, bites again. The neighbourhood **buffalo** chewing by the wall turns bubblegum pink, nose to tail. It looks down at itself and looks deeply unimpressed. *Mooooo.*

Off-screen, the kids roar with laughter. Kabir bows.

Then he looks at his hand.

His fingertips **glitter**. They have turned to crystal sugar: clear and hard, catching the sun. He taps one on the jar. *Tink.*

The laughter goes on, but Kabir's smile has gone. He shoves his hand into his pocket.

---

## 7. LOW POINT — 12:30–15:00

**EXT. ROOFTOP — EVENING**

Dinner on the charpai. Kabir eats left-handed. His right hand is wrapped in a thick winter glove.

> **NANI**
> Gloves? In this heat?

> **KABIR**
> It's... fashion.

Meher watches him. Kabir won't look at her.

**EXT. BLUE WALL — MIDNIGHT**

Meher and Chutney wait. Kabir comes up quietly behind them, the jar of jalebis clutched to his chest. Nearly all the coils are gone.

The clock strikes. The cracks glow... **barely**. The shop's outline flickers in the wall like a bad bulb. Half an awning. One lantern. A door that is mostly an idea.

**INT. SHOP — CONTINUOUS**

Nearly bare. A handful of jars give a weak glow, and dust hangs in the dark. Tola is dull and tarnished, its eyes half shut.

Behind the counter there is almost nothing left of Amma: a pair of spectacles, the shimmer of a shawl, a voice.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> Ah. Both of you.

Kabir puts the jar on the counter. His voice cracks.

> **KABIR**
> I took it. I wanted them to think I was... somebody.

He peels off the glove. Crystal sugar has crept past his knuckles to his wrist. It glints in the lantern-light.

> **KABIR**
> I'm sorry.

> **MISHRI AMMA** *(softly)*
> Oh, little man.

Kabir puts his crystal hand on Tola's pan.

> **KABIR**
> Then take my sorry.

A bubble begins to rise, flickers, and fades to nothing. A sorry is true, but it isn't a kindness *done*. Tola cannot move. Kabir's shoulders shake.

Meher turns to the faint shape of Amma.

> **MEHER**
> Amma, why are you fading?

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> A shop like this lives in people's remembering. When the last one forgets...

She shrugs. The shrug is almost invisible.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> ...poof. Sugar in the rain.

Meher's face changes. She is putting it together.

> **MEHER**
> Nani's story. She forgot the ending... and you started to fade.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> Who's Nani?

Meher grabs Kabir's good hand.

> **MEHER**
> We need Nani.

The lantern gutters. The shop folds itself flat into the wall like a book slammed shut, and the gali is dark again.

---

## 8. CLIMAX — 15:00–17:30

**EXT. ROOFTOP — THE NEXT NIGHT, NEAR MIDNIGHT**

Meher and Kabir kneel by Nani's charpai.

> **MEHER**
> Nani. Wake up. It's your story. It's real.

Nani blinks up at them, confused and gentle.

> **NANI**
> At my age? Down all those stairs?

Kabir picks up the torch.

> **KABIR**
> I'll take you, Nani.

**INT. STAIRWELL — NIGHT** (**KABIR'S SWAGGER**, slowed and tender)

Dark. Steep. Kabir goes first, walking backwards, the torch in his crystal hand, Nani's hand in his good one. One step. Wait. One step. Wait. The kid who never waits for anything waits for every step.

> **KABIR**
> One more, Nani. I've got you.

Behind them, Meher carries Nani's slippers like treasure.

**EXT. BLUE WALL — MIDNIGHT**

The clock strikes. The wall gives only the faintest flicker.

Nani stares at the peeling blue paint. She steps forward and lays her palm on it, and very quietly begins to hum the six notes of her lullaby.

The wall **hums them back**. Faint chimes, the same six notes.

Nani's breath catches. From under her hand, a bubble rises, the biggest yet, sepia and gold.

**MEMORY (INSIDE THE BUBBLE) — EXT. BLUE WALL — EIGHTY YEARS AGO**

The same wall, newly painted. A small girl with flowers in her plaits, YOUNG SHARDA, stands where Meher stands now. The shop beside her is fading, and Amma is fading with it. Young Sharda sings the lullaby to her, and the shop glows back.

**BACK TO SCENE**

> **NANI** *(a whisper)*
> I was the girl. I was the girl in the story.

The bubble drifts through the wall...

**INT./EXT. THE SHOP — CONTINUOUS**

...and settles on Tola's pan. **CLONNNG.**

The shop **BLAZES** back to life. The wall unfolds faster and brighter than ever. Lanterns flare. Jars refill with a sound like rain. Colours flood the frame: saffron, rose, pistachio, silver. Tola gleams like new.

And Amma is **solid**. She gasps, looks at her own hands, and laughs.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> There you are, Sharda.

> **NANI**
> I forgot you.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> Everybody forgets. The trick is remembering again.

Kabir steps up to Tola. He lays his crystal hand on the pan.

A bubble rises from it, and inside it, *tonight*: the dark stairs, the torch, one step at a time.

It drops onto the pan. Tola tips **all the way down**. **THUNK.** It is the heaviest yet. The jars ring like temple bells.

Kabir's hand tingles. The crystal melts from his wrist, his knuckles, his fingertips, until it is just a hand again. He flexes it and laughs, and his eyes are wet.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> Now *that*... is heavy.

Chutney howls along with the chimes, badly.

---

## 9. DAWN — 17:30–19:00

**EXT. BLUE WALL — FIRST LIGHT**

The sky turns rose. The shop begins to fold itself away, slowly and peacefully: lanterns dim, the awning rolls up, the shutters close.

Amma, solid now, beckons Meher close. She pulls a single glowing **thread** from her sugar shawl and ties it around Meher's wrist.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> The job of remembering. It's yours now.

> **MEHER**
> Will you be here next summer?

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> If somebody tells the story right.

Amma winks.

> **MISHRI AMMA**
> Don't let the story lose its ending.

The last panel folds in. Only a peeling blue wall is left in the dawn, and on it, for one second, a small golden handprint that fades.

**EXT. GALI — SUNRISE**

Three long shadows walk home up the lane: Nani in the middle, a child holding each of her hands, and a one-eared dog trotting ahead, very proud of everyone.

---

## 10. CODA — 19:00–20:00

TITLE CARD: *Next summer.*

**EXT. NANI'S ROOFTOP — NIGHT**

Power cut. Stars. The same charpais. Meher sits up against the pillows now, with two small cousins at her feet. Kabir leans on the parapet, listening, and Nani fans herself, smiling. The glowing thread is on Meher's wrist.

> **MEHER**
> ...and the shop stayed, as long as someone remembered it. That's the ending.

She glances at Nani. Nani hums the six notes.

> **MEHER**
> Well. That's how it ends. And that's how it begins.

**EXT. GALI — MIDNIGHT**

Down the dark lane, at the dead end, the blue wall **shimmers** once, gold in its cracks.

CUT TO BLACK. CREDITS.

**POST-CREDITS**

**INT. SHOP — NIGHT**

Chutney looks left. Chutney looks right. Chutney snaffles a floating laddoo from the counter...

...and slowly rises out of frame, legs paddling, ears (ear) flapping, looking deeply surprised.

**THE END**
