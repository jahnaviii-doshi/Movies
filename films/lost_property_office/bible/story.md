# PART A — CREATIVE BRIEF

## A1. Logline
A month after her grandfather dies, a girl who lost his old station-master's pocket watch boards a midnight train made of luggage tags to the Lost Property Office in the clouds — where everything ever lost is filed away, and you may only take back what you're truly ready to lose again.

## A2. The two layers
- **Kid layer:** a magical train, an infinite office of lost things (umbrellas, single socks, milk teeth, lost tempers in jars), a fussy head clerk, forms in triplicate, a lost kite who becomes a friend, a race against fading.
- **Adult layer:** grief. Holding on too tightly. The things we never said. The idea that what we love isn't kept by grasping it. The words "death", "died" and "grief" must never be spoken in the film. Handle it through the watch, the empty chair and Dadaji's line.

## A3. Influences (techniques only — story, characters and world are entirely original)
Classic British children's-fantasy techniques: a child travelling alone into a magical world next door to ordinary life; strict, slightly absurd rules run by eccentric adults; a ticking deadline; a safe return home with something gained.

## A4. Setting
**Real world:** a small Indian railway junction town — an old station with a red-and-cream building, a big clock that's always five minutes slow, a tea stall, a footbridge, and a disused siding beyond the last platform. Anaya's family home is near the station; Dadaji was the station master for 40 years. His chair on the veranda is now empty.
**The other world:** the Lost Property Office — a colossal building floating in moonlit clouds, a mix of Indian railway bureaucracy and endless library. Brass lamps, green-shaded desks, rubber stamps, ledgers taller than houses, pneumatic tubes, rolling ladders, shelves that rise until they dissolve into the night sky as stars. **Every clock in the Office is stopped — except, somewhere, one watch.**

## A5. Characters (keep cast small)
| Name | Who | Arc / function |
|---|---|---|
| **Anaya** (10) | Quiet, stubborn, fiercely loyal; short hair, Dadaji's too-big railway cardigan over her clothes | From grasping tightly to letting go — and from silence to saying it |
| **Dadaji** (flashbacks + voice) | Retired station master; white moustache, spectacles on a string, always winding his pocket watch | His line is the film's spine: *"Time is for spending, beta, not for keeping."* |
| **Bade Babu** | Head clerk of the Office — tall, thin, made of folded paper, owl-like spectacles, a rubber stamp for a hand; fussy, rule-bound, secretly kind | The rules, and the comedy of bureaucracy; the gatekeeper |
| **Chhota Babu** | Junior clerk — small, round, ink-stained paper, eager, always dropping files | Comic relief; helps Anaya break a few small rules |
| **Patang** | A lost paper kite, faded pink with a torn tail and a broken string; moves and gestures, no words | Her companion and mirror: lost too, longs to fly again |

## A6. World rules (introduce through action)
1. Everything ever lost ends up in the Office, filed and catalogued.
2. You can only get there if you've lost something that truly matters: the **Luggage-Tag Express** arrives at the disused siding at midnight for those people.
3. You may take back only what you're **truly ready to lose again**. Bade Babu's stamp simply will not come down otherwise.
4. Things never claimed slowly rise up the shelves and become stars.
5. Visitors who stay too long start to get lost themselves: a luggage tag appears on your wrist and your colours fade.

## A7. Beat sheet (target timings — runtime 20:00)
1. **00:00–02:00 — Before.** Warm flashback: Dadaji and little Anaya on the platform, he lets her wind the watch: "Time is for spending, beta, not for keeping." Cut to now: the house is quiet, his veranda chair empty, relatives speaking softly. Anaya tears her room apart looking for the watch she lost at the station.
2. **02:00–04:00 — The train.** Midnight. She sneaks to the station and, at the disused siding, a train built of thousands of fluttering luggage tags pulls in. Its doors open just for her.
3. **04:00–07:00 — The Office.** Arrival in the clouds: the big set-piece of infinite shelves. Bade Babu and his forms ("Form 7-B, Item Lost, Sentimental, in triplicate"). Comic tour: the Department of Single Socks, a shelf of lost milk teeth, jars of lost tempers rattling, a lost voice whispering from a drawer. Chhota Babu takes a shine to her.
4. **07:00–10:00 — The search.** The ledger says the watch is filed in a department far above. Journey up rolling ladders and through pneumatic tubes. She meets Patang, a lost kite tangled in a shelf, and frees it; it follows her. They pass the **Department of Things Never Said**: shelves of sealed glass jars, each holding a faint glowing whisper.
5. **10:00–13:00 — Found.** At the top, among the stopped clocks, the only ticking thing: Dadaji's watch. Anaya clutches it. Bade Babu arrives: "Are you ready to lose it again?" She says "Yes" — and the stamp will not come down. It knows.
6. **13:00–16:00 — Holding on.** She runs with the watch. The Office reacts: shelves begin dissolving into stars around her, a luggage tag appears on her wrist, her colours start to fade, and she's being filed as lost. Patang drags her out of a collapsing aisle — and tears itself further doing it. They tumble into the Department of Things Never Said. One jar glows with her own handwriting on the label: *"Anaya — to Dadaji."* Inside: the goodbye she never said.
7. **16:00–18:00 — Climax.** She opens the jar and says it out loud (short, simple, devastating; write it with restraint). The Office goes still. Her colour returns. She understands Dadaji's line. She looks at Patang, broken and longing for the sky, and makes a choice: she asks Bade Babu to let Patang be **found** instead, and returns the kite to the wind. Then she holds out the watch, and this time she truly means it. The stamp comes down: *RETURNED TO OWNER.*
8. **18:00–19:30 — Home.** Dawn at the station; the luggage-tag train dissolves into fluttering paper. The watch is ticking in her hand. Far away, a little boy in another town looks up as a faded pink kite drifts down into his hands. In the sky, a new star fades into the morning.
9. **19:30–20:00 — Coda.** On the veranda, Anaya teaches her small cousin to wind the watch — then puts it in his hands to keep. "Time is for spending." She sits in Dadaji's chair. Final insert: in the Office, Bade Babu writes in the ledger — *"One (1) heavy heart. Returned to owner. Lighter."* Credits.

## A8. Visual style
Soft watercolour-and-ink look with visible paper texture and gentle ink outlines; simple, rounded character designs with clear silhouettes.
**Two palettes:** the real world is dusty and muted — faded sepia, railway red and cream, pale morning blue — and the flashbacks are warm golden. The Office is moonlit paper-white, ink blue and polished brass, with stars glowing at the top of the shelves. Anaya's fading is shown by desaturation toward grey; her recovery returns full colour. Patang is the one bright pink note in the Office.
Camera: vast vertical scale in the Office (tiny Anaya, towering shelves); intimate, still framing at home; the watch shot as a hero object in close-up with shallow focus.

## A9. Sound & music
Instruments: solo piano, sarangi, soft strings, a music box.
Leitmotifs: **(1) Dadaji's theme** — a simple, warm melody first heard on the platform, played on the watch's chime as a music box; it returns in full at the climax. **(2) The Office** — playful, clockwork-pizzicato bureaucracy music for Bade Babu. **(3) Patang's theme** — airy, rising flute line.
**Ticking is a musical element:** the Office is silent of clocks except the watch's tick, which grows louder as she nears it and which the score locks to in the climax.
Soundscape: station announcements in the distance, the tea-seller's call, the fluttering of thousands of luggage tags, stamps thudding, pneumatic tubes whooshing.

## A10. Tone guardrails
This is the most emotional of the films — earn it with comedy first (the Office must be genuinely funny for the first half). No on-screen death, no funeral imagery, no illness. The grief lives in the empty chair, the watch and the unsaid goodbye. The climax line must be short and plain; resist melodrama. The ending is warm, not sad.
