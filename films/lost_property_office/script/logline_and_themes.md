# The Lost Property Office: logline and themes

## Logline
A month after her grandfather is gone, a girl who lost his old station-master's pocket watch boards a midnight train made of luggage tags to the Lost Property Office in the clouds, where everything ever lost is filed away and you may take back only what you're truly ready to lose again.

## Kid layer
- **A train** built from thousands of fluttering luggage tags that climbs into the sky.
- **An infinite office of lost things.** Shelves rise until they turn into stars. There are pneumatic tubes to ride, rolling ladders, a Department of Single Socks still waiting for their partners, rattling drawers of milk teeth, jars of lost tempers that escape on Mondays, and a lost voice in a drawer asking about its keys.
- **Two paper clerks.** Bade Babu is fussy, stamps everything and wants it in triplicate. Chhota Babu drops everything and is kind.
- **A lost pink kite,** Patang, who becomes her friend and saves her.
- **A race against fading:** a luggage tag ties itself to her wrist and her colours start to drain away.
- **A happy ending:** the kite flies free into a new child's hands, the watch is wound again, and the big red stamp says RETURNED TO OWNER.

## Adult layer
- **Grief, never named.** The film never speaks the word.
- **Holding on too tightly.** Anaya clutches the watch so hard she begins to be filed as lost herself. Gripping the thing we loved can stop us from living.
- **The things we never said.** A whole department of jars holds unsent thank-yous and sorrys. The goodbye she didn't get to say has been waiting on a shelf, and saying it out loud is what returns her colour.
- **Love kept by spending it.** Dadaji's line, *"Time is for spending, little one. Not for keeping,"* only makes sense once she gives the kite away and passes the watch to her small cousin. The station clock stays five minutes slow, because everyone could use five extra minutes.
- **What changes, quietly.** The empty chair is filled not by replacing him, but by her sitting in it.

## Tone
Funny first: the bureaucracy jokes carry the first half. Then come stillness and one plain sentence, and the film ends warm rather than sad. There is no illness, no funeral and no on-screen loss. The grief lives entirely in the watch, the empty chair and the unsaid goodbye.

## The poster image
A tiny girl in an oversized cardigan stands at the foot of impossibly tall shelves that rise into a night sky. As the shelves climb, their contents turn into stars. She holds a glowing brass pocket watch up toward them. A faded pink kite with a torn tail flies at her shoulder, the only bright colour in the frame. Above her, far up the shelves, one star shines warmer and more golden than the rest.
