# THE LOST PROPERTY OFFICE

*An original animated short film. Runtime 20:00.*

**CAST**
ANAYA (10). Quiet, stubborn, fiercely loyal. Short choppy hair, a too-big grey-green railway cardigan with brass buttons, sleeves rolled three times; red canvas shoes.
DADAJI (70s, flashbacks only). Retired station master. Thick white moustache, round spectacles on a string, navy waistcoat, and always a brass pocket watch on a chain.
BADE BABU. Head clerk of the Office. Tall, thin, folded entirely from cream paper. Owl-round spectacles, a pencil behind one ear, and a rubber stamp where his right hand should be. Creases when he frowns.
CHHOTA BABU. Junior clerk. Small and round, made of crumpled blue paper spotted with ink. Always carrying too many files, always dropping them.
PATANG. A lost paper kite: faded pink, diamond-shaped, with a torn tail and a snapped string. It has no face, but it bows, flutters, droops and points.
CHINTU (5). Anaya's small cousin. Round, sticky-fingered, wears a yellow vest.
THE BOY (7). A boy in another town, far away. Blue shorts, one sock. Seen once.
MAA (O.S.). Anaya's mother. Heard, never seen up close.

---

## 1. BEFORE — 00:00–02:00

**EXT. RAILWAY PLATFORM — GOLDEN AFTERNOON (FLASHBACK)**

Everything glows warm, like old honey. A red-and-cream station building, a tea stall breathing steam, and a big platform clock showing 4:55.

Little ANAYA (6) sits on a bench beside DADAJI, swinging her legs. He takes out his brass pocket watch, opens it, and lets her hold it. It ticks in her small hands. **TICK. TICK. TICK.**

He guides her fingers to the winding crown. She winds. The watch lid plays a tiny tune when it closes. (**DADAJI'S THEME**, music-box)

> **DADAJI**
> Time is for spending, little one. Not for keeping.

She doesn't understand. She nods anyway, very seriously.

He taps the big platform clock and then his watch. They don't match.

> **DADAJI**
> That clock is five minutes slow. Has been for forty years. I never fixed it.

> **ANAYA (6)**
> Why?

> **DADAJI**
> Five extra minutes. Everyone could use five extra minutes.

A train whistle blows. The golden light flares white.

**INT. FAMILY HOUSE — VERANDA — DAY (NOW)**

Colour drains to dusty sepia. The same veranda, but quiet. A wooden armchair with a cane seat sits in the corner. It is empty, and a folded navy waistcoat rests on its arm.

Through the doorway, soft voices of relatives murmur and stop. A cup clinks. Somebody sighs. We see only distant backs in the next room.

**INT. ANAYA'S ROOM — DAY**

Anaya, 10 now, is wrapped in Dadaji's too-big railway cardigan. She is tearing her room apart: drawers out, pillows thrown, the mattress lifted. She looks under the bed, inside her shoes, and in a tin of marbles.

> **MAA (O.S.)** *(gently, from the hall)*
> Anaya? Food's ready.

Anaya doesn't answer. She sits on the floor among the mess, breathing hard. She rolls up a sleeve and looks at her empty wrist, where she must have once worn the watch on its chain like a bracelet.

**MEMORY FLASH — EXT. PLATFORM — DAY (QUICK)**

The crowded platform, a week ago. Anaya runs for a train, and the watch slips from her pocket. Its glint falls under the platform's edge.

**BACK TO SCENE**

Anaya presses her forehead to her knees.

From somewhere far away, so faint we might imagine it: **tick... tick... tick.**

---

## 2. THE TRAIN — 02:00–04:00

**INT. ANAYA'S ROOM — NIGHT**

Dark. Anaya lies awake. The ticking is still there, faint, coming from the window. She sits up.

**EXT. STATION — NIGHT**

Anaya slips through the gate in her cardigan and red shoes. The station is empty: a sleeping dog, the tea stall shuttered, and the big clock glowing. It says 11:55. The minute hand clicks on, very loudly, to midnight minus four.

She crosses the footbridge. Below her, the tracks shine in the moonlight like silver ribbon.

**EXT. DISUSED SIDING — CONTINUOUS**

Beyond the last platform: weeds, a rusted buffer stop, and a track that goes nowhere.

The platform clock behind her strikes midnight.

A wind rises out of nowhere. Something flutters in the dark: hundreds of small pale shapes swirling like moths.

**LUGGAGE TAGS.** Thousands of them, cardboard with string loops, tumbling down the track and clicking together. Tags lock onto tags. They become wheels, then an engine, then carriages with windows. Their strings stream from the roof like smoke.

**THE LUGGAGE-TAG EXPRESS** stands at the siding, rustling softly, as if breathing.

Anaya steps back. A carriage door, made of a hundred tags, folds open right in front of her and no one else.

A single tag flutters down and lands at her feet. It is blank, except for her name.

She looks back at the town once. Then she climbs aboard.

**INT. LUGGAGE-TAG EXPRESS — NIGHT**

Paper seats. Paper windows that rustle. The train lurches, and through the windows the station drops away, because the train is climbing. The rooftops go by, then the clouds, then the moon, very close and very big.

Anaya presses her palm to the window. Far ahead, something enormous floats among the clouds, lit up like a city.

---

## 3. THE OFFICE — 04:00–07:00

**EXT. THE LOST PROPERTY OFFICE — NIGHT**

A colossal building floating in moonlit cloud: part railway station, part library, part cathedral. Arched windows glow lamp-yellow. A clock tower rises above it, and its clock face is stopped. Shelves are visible through the windows, rising and rising until the tops of the building fade into stars.

The Express sighs to a halt at a cloud platform. A painted sign shows a pointing hand.

**INT. OFFICE — MAIN HALL — CONTINUOUS** (**THE OFFICE**, clockwork pizzicato)

Vast. Green-shaded brass lamps on long desks. Ledgers taller than houses lean against the walls. Pneumatic tubes snake everywhere, going *thwoomp* and *fssst* as canisters shoot past. Every clock on every wall is stopped.

At a high desk sits BADE BABU, folded from paper, stamping forms. *THUD. THUD. THUD.* He does not look up.

> **BADE BABU**
> Name.

> **ANAYA**
> Anaya. I lost a—

> **BADE BABU**
> Form Seven-B. Item Lost, Sentimental. In triplicate.

Three identical forms slide out of his desk like tongues.

> **ANAYA**
> Triplicate?

> **BADE BABU**
> Three. One for the Office, one for the Department, and one to be lost.

He stamps the third copy. It drifts off into the dark, gone. He nods with satisfaction.

A crash. A tower of files topples, and from behind it stumbles CHHOTA BABU, crumpled blue and ink-spotted, files raining around him.

> **CHHOTA BABU**
> Sorry, sorry, sorry! Visitor! A visitor! We haven't had a visitor since... since...

He looks at a stopped clock and gives up.

> **BADE BABU**
> Chhota Babu. Escort the visitor to the ledger. Touch nothing.

Chhota Babu touches everything.

**INT. OFFICE — CORRIDORS — CONTINUOUS (COMIC TOUR)**

They walk past departments, each with a small arched entrance.

**THE DEPARTMENT OF SINGLE SOCKS.** Endless shelves of socks without partners: striped, spotted, a tiny baby sock. Every one of them leans hopefully toward the door.

> **CHHOTA BABU**
> Very sad department. Always waiting.

**LOST MILK TEETH.** Tiny glass drawers rattle like maracas as they pass. One drawer slides open, a tooth bows politely, and the drawer shuts.

**LOST TEMPERS.** Jars on a shelf jump and rattle furiously, each with a little red storm inside. One lid pops, and a tiny red cloud zooms out, huffing. Chhota Babu catches it in a fresh jar and screws the lid down, panting.

> **CHHOTA BABU**
> Mondays.

Anaya almost smiles, and catches herself.

**A LOST VOICE.** As they pass a filing cabinet, one drawer whispers:

> **WHISPER (O.S.)**
> ...has anyone seen my... keys...

Chhota Babu shushes the drawer kindly and closes it.

**INT. OFFICE — THE GREAT LEDGER — CONTINUOUS**

A ledger the size of a house. Chhota Babu climbs a rolling ladder and turns a page with his whole body. A finger of ink runs down the columns.

> **CHHOTA BABU**
> Pocket watch. Brass. Station master's. Ticking.

He stops and stares at the entry.

> **CHHOTA BABU**
> *Ticking.* Nothing ticks up here.

He points straight up. The shelves rise so high that the top floors are only tiny lights, the lights become stars, and the stars become the sky.

> **CHHOTA BABU**
> Filed under "Things That Can't Be Replaced." Top floor.

Anaya looks up and up. Somewhere very far above, faint but sure: **TICK... TICK...**

---

## 4. THE SEARCH — 07:00–10:00

**INT. OFFICE — THE STACKS — NIGHT**

Anaya climbs a rolling ladder. Chhota Babu shoves it along a shelf to give her a ride, and it rolls, squeaking, past a hundred lost umbrellas. Anaya clings on, her stomach left behind. She is nearly enjoying it.

They reach a pneumatic tube station: a round brass hatch with a sign showing an arrow pointing UP.

> **CHHOTA BABU**
> Quickest way. Hold your breath.

> **ANAYA**
> Why?

*FWOOOMP.* She's sucked in.

**INT. PNEUMATIC TUBE — CONTINUOUS**

Anaya rockets up through a glass tube in a swirl of papers, shelves blurring past outside. Her cardigan billows. She shoots up past whole departments: lost marbles, lost kites, lost birthdays.

**INT. OFFICE — UPPER STACKS — CONTINUOUS**

*POP.* She lands on a dusty walkway in a puff of paper. Chhota Babu arrives a second later, upside down, with his files in a fan.

It is quieter up here. The light is bluer. The shelves are taller.

Something rustles. Caught in the corner of a shelf, tangled in its own string, is a **pink paper kite**. It struggles weakly and gives up, its torn tail drooping.

Anaya kneels. Carefully, she untangles the knotted string, wrapping it around her fingers. The kite comes free. It hangs in the air in front of her, unsure.

Then it bows. (**PATANG'S THEME**, airy flute)

> **ANAYA**
> You're lost too.

The kite shivers along its whole body. Yes.

> **CHHOTA BABU** *(reading its tag)*
> "Kite. Pink. Lost in a high wind. Never claimed." Oh. It's been up here a long time.

As Anaya walks on, the kite floats after her, at a respectful distance at first, then closer. She calls it **PATANG**. It does a loop.

**INT. OFFICE — DEPARTMENT OF THINGS NEVER SAID — CONTINUOUS**

They pass a long gallery lit by a soft, sad glow. Shelf after shelf holds sealed glass jars, and in each jar a faint light pulses like a heartbeat. If you lean close, each one whispers.

> **WHISPER (O.S., from a jar)**
> ...I should have said thank you...

> **WHISPER (O.S., another)**
> ...I'm sorry about the bicycle...

> **WHISPER (O.S., another)**
> ...you were right...

Anaya stops. She doesn't want to look, and she looks anyway.

> **CHHOTA BABU** *(quietly, for once)*
> We don't stop here. Come on.

Above them, louder now: **TICK. TICK. TICK.**

---

## 5. FOUND — 10:00–13:00

**INT. OFFICE — THE CLOCK ROOM — NIGHT**

The very top. An enormous round room under a glass dome full of stars. Clocks cover every wall: grandfather clocks, cuckoo clocks, alarm clocks, wristwatches in their hundreds, all stopped at different times. The silence is total.

Except for one sound. **TICK. TICK. TICK.**

On a small velvet cushion in the centre of the room, glinting: **DADAJI'S WATCH.**

Anaya runs. She grabs it and holds it to her chest with both hands. It ticks against her heart. (**DADAJI'S THEME**, on the watch's chime)

She opens the lid. The tiny tune plays. Her face crumples and holds together at the same time.

Patang hovers close, its tail curling around her shoulder.

*THUD.* Behind her, BADE BABU unfolds out of the shadows like a letter being opened. He has come up the hard way, and he is not out of breath, because paper doesn't breathe.

> **BADE BABU**
> Item located. One pocket watch. Very good.

He produces Form Seven-B and smooths it on a desk. He raises his stamp-hand.

> **BADE BABU**
> One question, as regulations require.

He peers at her over his owl spectacles.

> **BADE BABU**
> Are you ready to lose it again?

> **ANAYA**
> Yes.

The stamp comes down...

...and stops a finger's width above the paper. It strains, trembling. It will not go down.

Bade Babu frowns at his own hand. He pushes. Nothing.

> **BADE BABU** *(gently)*
> The stamp doesn't believe you.

> **ANAYA**
> It's MINE. It was HIS. I'm not losing it again. I'm not losing anything again!

She clutches the watch and **runs.**

---

## 6. HOLDING ON — 13:00–16:00

**INT. OFFICE — UPPER STACKS — NIGHT** (**THE OFFICE** theme, now in a minor key, faster)

Anaya runs down the long aisles, the watch in her fist. Patang streaks after her.

Behind her, the Office begins to react. Shelves shudder. Lost umbrellas open by themselves. Unclaimed things float off their shelves and rise, glowing, becoming stars as they go up.

> **BADE BABU (O.S., echoing)**
> Visitors who stay too long... get lost themselves!

Anaya looks down. A **luggage tag** has appeared on her wrist, tied with string. It wasn't there a second ago.

She tears at it. It won't come off.

Her red shoes are turning **grey**. The grey creeps up her socks and into the hem of the cardigan. Her colour is draining, like a photo left in the sun.

**INT. OFFICE — COLLAPSING AISLE — CONTINUOUS**

The shelves ahead tilt and buckle. Books, boxes and coats come tumbling down, dissolving into light. The floor tilts. Anaya slips toward the edge, where the aisle drops away into nothing but stars.

**PATANG** dives. It hooks its snapped string around her wrist and pulls. It is just paper, and it pulls with everything it has.

**RRRIP.** Its tail tears further. Its corner tears. But Anaya swings clear, and they tumble together through an archway—

**INT. DEPARTMENT OF THINGS NEVER SAID — CONTINUOUS**

—and land among the glowing jars. Quiet again. The whispers murmur all around.

Anaya sits up, grey to the elbows now. The tag on her wrist flutters. Patang lies crumpled beside her, torn, barely lifting.

> **ANAYA**
> Patang. Patang, I'm sorry.

The kite lifts one corner, weakly. It's fine. It is not fine.

Anaya looks up. On the shelf right in front of her, one jar glows brighter than all the others. It glows warm gold, the colour of the flashback platform.

The label is in her own handwriting:

***Anaya — to Dadaji.***

She stops breathing. Inside the jar, a small light flickers, and the whisper in it is in her own voice, too faint to make out.

---

## 7. CLIMAX — 16:00–18:00

**INT. DEPARTMENT OF THINGS NEVER SAID — CONTINUOUS** (silence; only the watch: **TICK. TICK. TICK.**)

Anaya stands, slowly. She takes the jar down. It is warm.

She sets Dadaji's watch beside it on the shelf, so that she can hold the jar with both hands. That alone costs her something.

She unscrews the lid. The light rises out, small and gold, and hangs in front of her face.

She looks at it for a long moment. Then she says it out loud, plainly, to no one and to him.

> **ANAYA**
> Bye, Dadaji.
> *(beat)*
> I love you.
> *(beat)*
> Thank you for the time.

The light drifts up, past the shelves, past the lamps, and becomes a single new **star** in the dark above.

**THE OFFICE GOES STILL.** Every whisper stops. The pneumatic tubes stop. The ticking stops too, just for one breath, and then starts again, gently.

Colour floods back into Anaya: red shoes, green-grey cardigan, brown skin, the blush in her cheeks. The luggage tag on her wrist unties itself and flutters away.

**DADAJI'S THEME** plays in full for the first time, carried by strings and piano, with the ticking of the watch locked inside its rhythm.

Anaya picks up the watch. She opens it. It ticks. She closes it, and the chime plays.

> **ANAYA** *(to herself)*
> Not for keeping.

*Shff.* Bade Babu unfolds at the end of the aisle, with Chhota Babu peeking out from behind him and clutching an armful of files.

Anaya looks at Patang: torn, crumpled, longing. Its one remaining tail ribbon lifts toward a high window, where a breath of real wind gets in.

> **ANAYA**
> Bade Babu. Can something be found instead of claimed?

Bade Babu considers this deeply. He produces a form. He reads it. He turns it over. He nods.

> **BADE BABU**
> Form Twelve. "Released to the Wind."

He stamps it. *THUD.* The stamp comes down at once.

Anaya carries Patang to the high window, with Chhota Babu pushing a ladder along to help. She smooths its torn paper and holds it up to the wind.

> **ANAYA**
> Go on. Someone's waiting.

She lets go. Patang hesitates, then catches the wind, and **flies.** It loops once around the clock tower, the way it did for her, and sails away into the night. (**PATANG'S THEME**, soaring)

Anaya turns back to Bade Babu. She holds out the watch on her open palm.

> **ANAYA**
> I'm ready to lose it again.

This time she means it. We can see it.

He raises the stamp. It comes down easily, softly, the way you'd close a book you loved.

**THUD.**

On the form, in red ink: **RETURNED TO OWNER.**

---

## 8. HOME — 18:00–19:30

**EXT. DISUSED SIDING — DAWN**

The Luggage-Tag Express sighs in at the siding. Anaya steps down onto real gravel in real morning light.

Behind her, the train breaks apart into thousands of fluttering tags that scatter into the sky like birds and are gone.

The watch is in her hand, ticking. **TICK. TICK. TICK.**

The station clock across the tracks says 5:55. It is still five minutes slow. Anaya smiles at it.

**EXT. A FIELD IN ANOTHER TOWN — MORNING**

Far away. A small, flat town, a mustard field, a water tower. THE BOY (7), in one sock, sits glumly on a wall.

Something pink drifts down out of the sky, turning slowly, and lands in his hands: a faded pink kite with a torn tail.

He stares at it. He looks up at the sky. He runs, and the kite lifts behind him, flying.

**EXT. STATION — MORNING**

Anaya walks home along the platform as the tea-seller opens his shutters. High above the station, in the pale morning sky, one last star is still shining.

It fades softly into the blue.

---

## 9. CODA — 19:30–20:00

**EXT. FAMILY HOUSE — VERANDA — AFTERNOON**

Warm light, nothing like the dusty sepia from before. CHINTU (5) sits on the veranda floor. Anaya sits beside him. She shows him the watch and guides his sticky fingers to the crown. He winds it, and it chimes.

> **ANAYA**
> Time is for spending.

She closes his hand around the watch. It's his to keep now. Chintu looks at it as if it's the most important thing in the world, and it is.

Anaya stands up, and with no ceremony, she sits down in **Dadaji's chair.** It creaks, as it always did. She pulls the cardigan around her, and it almost fits.

**INT. THE LOST PROPERTY OFFICE — NIGHT**

Lamp-light. The great ledger. Bade Babu writes in it with a long pen, slowly and with care.

We read over his shoulder:

***One (1) heavy heart. Returned to owner. Lighter.***

He blots it. He almost smiles, and the paper creases at the corners of his mouth.

CUT TO BLACK. CREDITS.

**THE END**
